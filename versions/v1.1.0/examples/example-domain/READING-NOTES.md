## The old favourite (thinking clearly) - 5/5

Notes written after the first read.

**Core idea: the map is not the territory**

The central claim is that the mental models we carry are simplified versions of reality, not reality itself. The failure mode is treating the map as the territory - making decisions based on your model of a situation rather than the actual situation.

Three practical consequences the book draws from this:

- Update your model when evidence contradicts it. This sounds obvious; the book makes the case that most people almost never do it deliberately, and gives a method for noticing when you are resisting an update.
- Ask "what would have to be true for me to be wrong about this?" before acting on a confident belief. This is a forcing function - it requires actually engaging with the contrary case rather than noting it exists and moving on.
- Distinguish between uncertainty in the world (genuine randomness) and uncertainty in your knowledge (you simply do not have the information yet). They require different responses.

**Why this earned a notes entry**

The map/territory distinction is something that comes up several times a week now - when forming an opinion from a summary, when planning based on an assumption that has not been checked, when disagreeing with someone and realising the argument is with a model of their position rather than what they actually said. Frequency of application is the test.
